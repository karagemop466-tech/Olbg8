Baseball Betting Markets Explained
Baseball tips on this page cover three markets for individual games: Money Line, Run Line and Game Totals. These are the same across MLB, NPB, KBO and World Baseball Classic games, so once you know them, you can follow our baseball predictions. We also offer ante-post tips on the winner of a league or tournament.

Money Line
The Money Line is the simplest baseball market: you pick the team you think will win. The margin does not matter, only the result. Because the favourite's odds are usually short, many bettors turn to the other markets or combine Money Line selections in accumulators.
Run Line
The Run Line is baseball's version of a handicap bet. The favourite is usually given a 1.5-run disadvantage and the underdog a 1.5-run head start. A favourite must therefore win by two runs or more for a Run Line bet on them to win, while a bet on the underdog wins if they win the game or lose by just one run. As baseball games are often decided by a single run, this market can offer very different odds from the Money Line on the same team.
Game Totals
Game Totals is an over/under market on the total runs scored by both teams. The bookmaker sets a line, for example, 8.5 runs, and you decide whether the game will produce more or fewer. The starting pitchers, the ballpark and the weather all influence the total, which is why the pitching stats covered further down this page are so useful for this market.
Extra innings and ties
Extra innings are usually included when bets are settled, so a game that goes beyond nine innings still counts in full. Rules differ by bookmaker, and in NPB and KBO regular-season games can end level after a set number of extra innings, so check how your bookmaker treats a tie before you bet.
Ante-post tips
An ante-post tip is a prediction on who will win a league or tournament, made before the competition is over rather than on a single game. The bet settles when the winner is decided, which can be weeks or months after you place it, so your stake is tied up for longer than it would be on a game bet. Terms vary by bookmaker, so check the rules before you bet.

Action vs Listed Bets in Baseball Betting
Baseball bets are often tied to the starting pitchers, so many bookmakers let you choose between an action bet and a listed bet on the Money Line, Run Line and Game Totals markets. The rules vary by bookmaker, so check their terms before you bet.
Action bets
If you take an action bet, your bet stands whoever starts the game. Starting pitchers are sometimes changed late, for example because of injury or illness, and an action bet stays in play when that happens. The odds you took are not affected, but they may differ from the listed price, so it is worth comparing both.
