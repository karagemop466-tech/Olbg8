How every line on this site is originated

OLBG publishes tip counts and confidence percentages but no numeric odds on its win markets. Rather than hunt for prices that do not exist, every line here is originated in-house from a stated anchor, an explicitly declared prior, and a published dispersion where one could be sourced.

1. The anchor

OLBG defines its own confidence percentage verbatim: “The selection odds are shown alongside the number of tips received compared with the total number of tips on all outcomes in the market, which gives the confidence percentage.” That makes the tip share across all outcomes of one market a normalised crowd distribution. It sums to one, so there is no overround to strip and no bookmaker margin to remove — it can be used directly as an anchor.

2. Shrinkage

A share built from two tips is not a probability. Each market is shrunk toward its prior with a Dirichlet-multinomial posterior mean, p_i = (n_i + k·π_i) / (N + k). The strength k is set per sport by how much scoring variance a single tip can reasonably carry: 4 for field sports, 6 for rugby, volleyball, handball and Gaelic, 8 for basketball, hockey, cricket, tennis, snooker and darts, 10 for baseball.

Priors are declared only where a sourced basis exists. Otherwise the prior is uniform and the shrinkage is purely a sample-size correction that makes no claim about team quality.

3. Probability to price

American odds are -100·p/(1-p) above evens and +100·(1-p)/p below. A book price adds a 4.5% overround. Both the fair and the book price are carried, and the band gate of -250 to +10000 is applied to our own originated price, never to OLBG’s crowd percentage.

4. Handicaps and totals

The expected margin is (p - 0.5) × margin_per_prob_point, and the fair handicap is that margin negated and rounded to the sport’s quoting increment. Cover, push and lose are computed as an exact disjoint partition, so push mass is never counted twice. Totals use the same partition against a sourced dispersion.

Where a sport’s margin can only take a handful of integer values the normal fit is wrong, and the price is settled against the published distribution instead. A best-of-11 snooker margin is one of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 and never anything near zero: at p=0.70 the normal path gives 0.412 for a -1.5 handicap where the exact answer is 0.533, which is P(win) × 0.761.

5. Confidence

Confidence is not a restatement of how short a price is. A -800 favourite is not a high-confidence pick; it is a low-return one. Because the target band is -250 to +10000, “high” here means well evidenced at a price that still offers a return.

It rises with the number of independent sourced strands pointing the same way. A crowd-only pick can never be high: OLBG’s own tipsters logged a 37.5% strike rate across 948,262 settled tips in the twelve months to 17 September 2026, so an uncorroborated tip share is a weak anchor however lopsided it looks. A thin crowd sample caps confidence only when the crowd share is what the probability rests on — a pick derived from sourced market prices and full-season counts is not weakened by OLBG having two tips on the match.

7. What is never done

  • No value is inferred or filled. If OLBG did not publish it, the cell is empty and says so.
  • No prediction reuses OLBG tipster prose.
  • No fixture is presented as upcoming unless its date was confirmed against a primary or league source.
  • No head-to-head or form figure is asserted where two sources contradict each other; the contradiction is published instead.
  • The words “model” and “verified” do not appear in any prediction, and the publishing gate refuses to emit one that does.
