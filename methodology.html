Method

How the prices and the prose are produced

No price on this site is copied from a tipster. Every fair line is computed from the captured market board, and every sentence in a write-up is assembled from a captured field.

1. Reverse-engineering the OLBG board

OLBG renders its tips feed server side. Each row carries the event name, an ISO start time, the selection, the market label, the price in decimal, fractional and American form, the tip record and a consensus percentage. Event pages add the rest of the market board: match winner, handicap or line, match total, set betting and period markets where the sport has them. This desk reads those fields directly; it does not infer a market from a page title.

2. Turning a price into a fair line

A quoted price contains the bookmaker margin. We remove it with the power method: find the exponent k so that the sum of each implied probability raised to k equals one. That is stricter on longshots than proportional de-vigging and leaves the fair probabilities summing to exactly 100%.

p_i = 1 / decimal_i        solve  Σ p_i^k = 1        fair_i = p_i^k / Σ p_j^k

Worked example from the captured board: a two-way market at 1.77 and 2.25 de-vigs to 56.1% and 43.9%, which is a fair line of -128 and +128 on a 1.77/2.25 quote.

3. Adding independent information

Where an official feed exists (MLB, NHL, Formula 1) the fair price is nudged by official form: last-ten record, points percentage, scoring differential and a home-advantage term. The nudge is capped at 3.5 percentage points and the caveat is printed on each row. Where no official feed is reachable, the fair line is the de-margined board and the row is labelled "de-margined OLBG board" so the reader can see the difference.

4. Confidence bands and the price window

Picks are restricted to fair prices between -250 and +10,000, which is the band where a stake has a defensible shape. Anything shorter than -250 is excluded even when the chance is high, because the return does not compensate for the risk.

5. Writing the prediction

Each write-up is at least thirty words and contains, in varying order: the fixture and venue, the two sides' official record and recent results where a feed exists, the relevant scoring numbers, the market price next to our fair price, and an explicit statement of the bet. A missing field produces no sentence rather than a guess, which is why some write-ups are shorter and carry a flag.

6. What the desk deliberately does not do

  • It does not restate a tipster's reasoning as its own.
  • It does not fill an injury or lineup gap with an inference; unavailable stays blank and is flagged.
  • It does not present a market-anchored line as an independent rating.
  • It does not publish a pick outside the price window, however confident the board looks.
