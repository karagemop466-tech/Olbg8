Pricing & evidence

How a finding becomes a pick.

Originate probabilities from sporting evidence first. Compare market prices second. Publish only when timing, settlement and the written reasoning are supported.

Current delivery boundary

This is a source-inspection dashboard and research implementation, not a completed daily prediction service. No sport has a trained, backtested production pricing adapter. Mathematical kernels are tested but are not evidence of forecast accuracy. Fixtures and market coverage are partial.

1. Find and match the event

OLBG is the primary source for its own listing IDs and market labels. The competition, organizer or governing body is the source for participants, date, start, format, venue and official results. Match event ID, participant identities, date and settlement period before joining sources. A directory link is never fixture confirmation.

2. Keep facts separate from estimates

Store the source URL, retrieved content, retrieval time and claim scope. Unknown form, statistics, injuries, non-runners and availability remain blank; absence of an injury article does not mean no injuries. An independently estimated probability is an estimate, not an official fact. The currently empty fair-line fields must not be filled with market-implied probabilities.

3. Originate and test

Fit competition-specific parameters from official historical results with a strictly chronological train/test split. Account for home advantage, opponents, format and relevant availability. Measure held-out calibration, Brier score, log loss and sample size. Record input evidence, code version and uncertainty for each line. None of this production fitting has yet been completed.

Research kernels in scripts/pricing.py implement fair odds, explicit push settlement, IID first-to score distributions, regulation Poisson scores and a full-field Gaussian ranking prototype. They require supplied parameters; no default sporting strengths or simulated examples are published as real picks. Cricket innings, tennis serve rules, scoring components, overtime, retirements and race dependencies require additional sport-specific work.

4. Compare prices without confusing popularity and probability

Fair decimal odds with no push equal 1 / p. For a refunded push, fair decimal odds equal (1 − p_push) / p_win. At decimal odds d, expected profit per unit stake is p_win × (d − 1) − p_loss. Mechanical no-vig market benchmarks normalize implied probabilities across mutually exclusive outcomes; they are not independently originated lines.

Eligible quoted American prices are −250 through +10000, excluding invalid magnitudes below 100. A −250 quote implies 71.43% before removing margin; +10000 implies 0.99%. A high chance of winning and positive betting value are different concepts. A 78% share of OLBG tips is neither a 78% win probability nor proof of value.

5. Confidence and editorial policy

Low, medium and high are reserved for calibrated, evidence-complete forecasts. No confidence tier is assigned in this snapshot. Production tier thresholds require backtest support, rather than declaring all favourites high confidence. The release gate also requires future start time, fresh odds (at most 30 minutes old under this project policy), confirmed market rules and positive expected return.

Each released high-confidence prediction must be original, at least 30 words, name an explicit selection and market, and cite official evidence for form, recent results, relevant statistics and any applicable availability information. Unsupported injury claims are omitted and missing evidence prevents release. Repeated boilerplate, copied OLBG comments and the words “model” and “verified” are prohibited in prediction text. There are no published prediction paragraphs yet.

6. Daily ranking and shortages

Aim for five distinct qualifying matches per sport, expandable to ten when evidence supports it. Rank eligible picks by calibrated confidence and value, then separate today from upcoming using authoritative UTC starts. Do not count multiple markets on one match as five matches. A shortage is shown as a shortage; races, tournaments and out-of-season categories cannot be invented to fill a quota.

7. Automated collection and boundaries

A separate artifact-only workflow attempts public category-link discovery daily. It does not update this public snapshot. It checks robots.txt, rate-limits requests and records access/markup failures. It does not enumerate undocumented APIs or bypass access controls. New observations go only to the collection log, never directly to picks. Official-stat ingestion, event matching, production pricing and narrative generation are not yet automated. No user data entry is required to view the dashboard, but the full autonomous prediction pipeline remains unfinished.
