The one pick that cleared every gate

India to win the New Zealand vs India 3rd T20 Match — win match at -205. Thirty-five of the fifty tips in this market sit on India, the single largest block of agreement on the whole multi-sport board. The official reporting backs why: India chased 226 and then 181 at Hagley Oval to win the first two games by four wickets apiece. New Zealand's pace depth has thinned, with Matt Henry removed from the series after bowling two overs in the second game and Lockie Ferguson unavailable for this match, while Ishan Kishan's adductor strain keeps Vaibhav Sooryavanshi in the India XI. India to win the match at -205, with Wellington's wind the only real argument against.

A High label needs all four of: an official source opened and matched to the fixture, a priced selection at 60% or better, at least four tips in that market, and a line inside −250 to +10,000. Fourteen of today's events met the first condition; the crowd-weighted price is what decides the rest.

Irregularities found in the repository, and what we did

  • Fabricated darts file removed. data/darts.json held five "verified: True" darts picks that cited a tournament directory page and quoted statistics the PDC does not publish. The fixtures it named do not appear in any capture file. Moved to quarantine/ with the evidence written up on the flagged page.
  • OLBG is reachable. Earlier session notes stopped at a TLS error from the sandbox and concluded nothing could be collected, which is why the site published zero picks. Direct retrieval works; today's board is the result.
  • OLBG's printed times are US Eastern, not UK time. Four events cross-checked against official start times land on UTC−4. Two others sit an hour off, so official start times are treated as authoritative and shown beside each row.
  • Consensus is not always right. Handball's most-tipped outcome is the draw on eight of fifteen markets, and on Kielce v Kolstad the crowd is on a tie against a side with a 7-0-0 head-to-head record. Those rows are published as counter-consensus reads, not copied.
