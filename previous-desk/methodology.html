Reverse-engineering the OLBG tips pages

How the site is put together, what the numbers on OLBG actually mean, and which markets exist per sport.

The structure of an OLBG listing

  1. Category page — olbg.com/betting-tips/<Sport>/<id>. One card per event, ordered by the number of tips on the most popular selection. It prints the event name, a relative time label, the selection, the market, “x/y Win Tips”, the confidence percentage, and counts of experts and comments.
  2. Event page — reached from the card. It shows “Most Popular Tip” as a complete table of every selection in the market with its own “x / y” and percentage, then repeats each market below with the same figures plus one tipster comment and that tipster's annual profit, strike rate, 30-day and 7-day numbers.
  3. What the percentage is. It is the share of tips on that outcome out of the tips on that market, not a win rate and not an implied probability from a bookmaker. OLBG says so in its own Value Rating definition, and the totals confirm it: on New Zealand v India the two sides are 35 and 15 of 50, which sums to the denominator.
  4. What is missing. The betslip price comparison is rendered after a click, so no bookmaker price exists in the readable markup. Every number in the “our line” column on this site therefore came from this desk, not from a book.

Time labels

OLBG prints “Today 21:00” style labels with no zone. Four fixtures checked against official start times all land on UTC−4 — Columbus at Philadelphia printed 19:07 against an official 23:00Z start, Boston at Carolina 20:07 against 00:00Z, Kielce 15:45 against a 19:45Z kick-off, and Wellington 03:00 against a 07:00Z start. Tampa Bay and the Mexican Grand Prix each sit an hour further out than that rule predicts, so the raw label is kept on every row, the official UTC time is quoted in words where it was found, and nothing on this site claims a kick-off time from OLBG's label alone.

What this site will not do

  • Publish a price for a market that had no tips. Two totals markets today (the World Series Game 5 line at 7.5 and the Kielce points total at 63.5) carry zero volume; both are left unpriced rather than filled from a prior.
  • Turn an outright into a confidence bet. Four sports' OLBG coverage is outrights only, and a share of a handful of tips across a 128-player field is a popularity index, not a price.
  • Treat an OLBG paragraph as a fact. Tipster comments are quoted as opinions attributed to OLBG's crowd, never restated as scouting.
  • Claim five picks a sport when the evidence covers three. Every shortfall is named on the overview ledger.
