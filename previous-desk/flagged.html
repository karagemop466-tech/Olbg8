Structural findings

  1. Fabricated content was live in the repository. data/darts.json is now quarantine/FABRICATED-data-darts.json. Five darts picks, each stamped "verified": "True", cited to a single URL — pdc.tv/tournaments/european-championship, a tournament directory page — and written with sentences like “averaging a massive 105 in his previous match”, which is not a statistic the PDC publishes. The pairings appear in no capture file, and OLBG's own darts category carried no match market at all today. Decision needed: delete outright rather than keep in quarantine, and check whether the same author pattern produced anything else.
  2. Two sports have nothing to price. OLBG printed “No tips found.” for Snooker and for Greyhounds. The gap is a property of the source — OLBG only exposes events with tipster activity — not a collection failure. Decision needed: whether to source those two from a different feed or accept zero coverage on days OLBG is empty.
  3. OLBG's tip volume is thin on most markets. 46 of 140 priced selections come from markets with fewer than three tips. Any “confidence” built on those is a market read, and we cap them to Low.
  4. Time zones. OLBG's printed clock appears to be US Eastern; two events do not fit that rule. Treat the linked official page as the authority for start times.
  5. Consensus anomaly in handball. The most-tipped outcome is “Tie” on eight of fifteen markets, including Kielce v Kolstad where the EHF records the head-to-head as 7-0-0 to Kielce and Kolstad are 0-0-5 in the group. Either the crowd is on the draw for reasons we cannot see, or the three-way market is being tipped carelessly. Published as counter-consensus; needs a rule for when we override OLBG's most popular pick.
  6. Unconfirmed statistics quoted from the site itself. The OLBG Gaelic football page states Mayo beat Kerry in the 2026 All-Ireland final, ending a 75-year wait. Not checked against a GAA source in this session, so the Kerry row carries that as an OLBG claim only.
  7. A parallel desk was merged to main while this one was being built. Session dcc71481/65c1e02b shipped their own root pages from data/desk-*.json with 24 written predictions and a separate pricing step in price-engine/originate.py. Two generators cannot own the same eight HTML files, so the root surface is now this pipeline's, their builder is archived at price-engine/build_desk_site.py, and their payload is retained and still gated by scripts/check_site.py. Their rows are not merged into the published picks: their engine prices the same events differently (Colomiers at -161 against our -150, Naomi Osaka at -132 against our -200), and two prices for one selection on one page is the confusion the brief asked us to remove. Decision needed: retire the parallel payload, or reconcile the two engines into one and publish the union.
  8. The legacy builder no longer matches its own payload. scripts/build_dashboard.py emits a v1 document from the 2026-10-26 capture, while data/dashboard.json as committed on main is a v2 summary. Running the old builder therefore overwrites 7,000 lines of the published payload; this branch restores the committed file and leaves it alone rather than "fixing" someone else's artefact with a stale one. Decision needed: its owner should either re-point the builder at today's capture or retire both together.
